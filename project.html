Disruptions to leading-strand synthesis impair and stall replication forks because DNA synthesis by the leading-strand polymerase, Pol ε, is essential for optimal template unwinding by the replicative CMG (CDC45–MCM–GINS) helicase1,2. Failure to efficiently restart and/or protect stalled replication forks is a major source of genomic instability3,4,5,6. Stalled forks can be restarted by a variety of mechanisms, including repriming downstream of the impediment4. In human cells, the primase–polymerase PRIMPOL promotes fork progression during replication stress7,8,9,10, but how its activity is coordinated by the replisome is unresolved
